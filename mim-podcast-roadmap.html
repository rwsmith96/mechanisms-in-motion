The Tech StackMechanisms in Motion

Four subscriptions, one job each

Each tool does exactly one thing and hands cleanly to the next: Riverside records, Descript edits, Transistor publishes, Calendly schedules. Buy each on annual billing and run them on one shared account so there is a single login per layer.
$24/mo
Riverside.fm
Pro plan · recording studio

Records every person's camera and mic locally (to their own computer, not just over the internet), so a guest's bad wifi can't ruin the take. Guests click one link in Chrome, no account, no download. One-click export hands each person to Descript as a separate track (each voice as its own file). Record at 1080p, the standard HD; 4K just makes files four times bigger for no visible gain.

$24/mo
Descript
Creator plan · editing

You edit the video by editing the transcript. One click cleans the audio (Studio Sound), removes filler words (the "ums"), and switches between cameras automatically (Automatic Multicam). It also generates the vertical clips for social. Annual billing is $24/mo; month-to-month is $35.

$49/mo
Transistor.fm
Professional · hosting

Your one upload puts the show in every podcast app and auto-posts the full video to your YouTube channel. Unlimited shows on one bill, so a second AES show later costs nothing extra.

$15/mo
Calendly
Standard · booking

Guests self-book a recording slot; it detects their timezone and emails them the Riverside link, the prep one-pager, and reminders automatically. One shared booking calendar avoids double-booking.

How an episode reaches the world

Guaranteed path (set up once, runs itself)

Audio: Transistor → one RSS feed → Apple Podcasts + Spotify + every podcast app, automatically on each publish. (An RSS feed is the standard format podcast apps watch for new episodes; Transistor manages it, which is why one upload reaches every app.)   Video: Transistor auto-posts the full episode to YouTube, the primary home for video discovery (about a third of US podcast viewing).

Bonus path (only if it's live on the account)

Native video on Apple Podcasts and Spotify ships through Transistor's HLS distribution (a format that plays video inside those apps, not just audio), still rolling out in 2026. Treat it as a bonus we switch on if available, never something the launch depends on. YouTube is the guaranteed video home.

"Record once, clip many"

The full edited episode is the master. From it: the audio goes to every podcast app automatically, the full video lands on YouTube, and Descript finds the best moments and generates 2–4 short vertical clips (phone-screen format) for YouTube Shorts, LinkedIn, and Instagram, from which you keep the best. Audio still reaches roughly twice the weekly US audience of video, so the show never waits on video to ship.

The one dollar that matters most

The microphone. If anything has to flex, keep the MV7+ and trade down elsewhere. We run it in "Auto Level" mode with light denoise and reverb off (reverb is an artificial-echo effect; keep it off). A pro-mixer upgrade path exists later but is not needed now.

Guest uplift kit (optional, for marquee guests only)

Guests are the weakest link in remote audio and video; a senior operator on a bad laptop webcam will be the most-watched face in the episode. For high-value guests, offer to ship a tiny uplift kit ahead of the record: a clip-on light and a USB mic or clip-on lavalier mic (about $25–60 total). Everyone else uses what they have; the platform protects the audio.

The Production WorkflowMechanisms in Motion

Record clean, edit by text, ship to spec

Quality is decided at record time, not in the edit. Get separate local tracks and matched settings on the way in, then Descript does the heavy lifting on the way out. Two rules prevent almost every disaster: confirm the real mic is selected, and never close the tab until uploads finish.

Session-day checklist recording how-to

  • Both hosts join 10 min early in Chrome
  • In Riverside, select the MV7+ as the mic (not the laptop default)
  • Confirm "separate tracks / local recording" is ON (each voice saved as its own file)
  • Headphones on for everyone (kills echo)
  • Both hosts run a QuickTime audio backup (free local safety copy)
  • Hit Record, say the slate ("Episode N, take 1"), pause 3 seconds silent (gives the editor a point to line up tracks)
  • Record the conversation
  • At the end, STAY on the page until every track shows 100% uploaded
If a guest's audio is rough

Ask them, live: move closer to the screen, turn off fans/AC, put on headphones. Do it in the warm-up minutes, not in the edit.

The Descript edit (4–6 hrs early, 2–3 later) edit how-to

  1. Import the multitrack (the recording with each person on their own track)
  2. Apply Studio Sound (one-click noise cleanup) per track at 60–80% (not 100%, it gets robotic)
  3. Edit by deleting text; then Remove Filler Words, keeping a few natural ones
  4. Run Automatic Multicam (auto camera-switching), then fix the obvious miscuts
  5. Add captions (auto-synced from the transcript)
  6. Generate 2–4 vertical clips; keep the best; hook in first 2 seconds
  7. Export video for YouTube and audio for Transistor

Export to spec: the same numbers every time what these mean, in plain English

In plain English: make every episode the same loudness as other professional shows, sharp HD video, and identical audio in the YouTube and podcast versions. Descript's export presets do almost all of this for you; the numbers below are just the targets. Every term decoded here.

Loudness: −14 LUFS (how loud; matches Spotify/YouTube)
True peak: −1.0 dBTP (a safety ceiling so audio never distorts)
Video: 1080p, 30fps, H.264 MP4 (HD video) → YouTube
Audio: stereo MP3/AAC, 48kHz (podcast audio) → Transistor
Frame rate: 30fps for everyone (keeps lips in sync)
Same cleaned audio in the video and the podcast file

Pre-publish QA gate (a 30-second check before each episode goes out): (1) confirm the export reads −14 LUFS / −1.0 dBTP (Descript shows it); (2) play the video at the start, middle, and last minute and check the mouths match the words; (3) confirm captions still match at the end. Scheduling & Guest OpsMechanisms in Motion

Guests self-book; the system preps them

One Calendly event and one prep doc run the entire guest journey. Carla sources and invites the guest; the guest books their own slot; Calendly handles timezones and sends everything they need. The tech check happens in the first five minutes of the recording.

The one Calendly event how to build it

  • Name: "Mechanisms in Motion: Recording"
  • Length: 75 min (60 of conversation + warm-up + slack)
  • Buffers: 15 min before and after (so you're never rushed or double-booked)
  • Location: the Riverside studio link (Riverside's one permanent room link); paste it once and every booking carries it
  • Availability: a dedicated block, Tue/Thu mornings Pacific, capped at 1–2 recordings/day; no Saturdays
  • Intake questions: headline/title, LinkedIn or bio link, one Amazon-mechanism story they want to tell
  • Owner: one shared booking calendar; the co-host is a guest on the invite

The prep one-pager (one doc, reused)

  • The show premise and the "Day 1 Energy" tagline
  • The 5–6 question arc for their episode
  • Format: conversational, ~60 min, video on
  • The 4-line tech checklist: laptop + Chrome, headphones on, wired internet (an Ethernet cable to the router) or sit near the router, close Slack/email/notifications
  • "We record locally, so a dropped connection won't ruin the audio. If you glitch, keep talking."
  • What to bring: one or two stories, a metric or two

Release cadence

Record the 8 plus one hosts-only evergreen (a no-guest episode held in reserve), then publish weekly on a fixed day ("new episodes every Tuesday"). Launch gate: do not launch until 4 episodes are fully edited and QA-passed; editing, not recording, is the real constraint. Never publish below a one-episode buffer; if it hits zero, pause the streak on purpose rather than ship unedited. Use Transistor's scheduled-publish (queue episodes to go live on future dates automatically) to load the season, and start booking Season 2 in mid-October so the show never runs out of episodes.
