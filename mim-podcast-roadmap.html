The Tech StackMechanisms in Motion

Four subscriptions, one job each

Each tool does exactly one thing and hands cleanly to the next: Riverside records, Descript edits, Transistor publishes, Calendly schedules. Buy each on annual billing and run them on one shared account so there is a single login per layer.
$24/mo
Riverside.fm
Pro plan · recording studio

Records every person's camera and mic locally at full quality, then uploads in the background, so a guest's bad wifi can't ruin the take. Guests click one link in Chrome, no account, no download. One-click export hands each person to Descript as a separate track. Record at 1080p (not 4K) to keep uploads fast.

$24/mo
Descript
Creator plan · editing

You edit the video by editing the transcript. One click cleans the audio (Studio Sound), removes filler words, and switches between cameras automatically (Automatic Multicam). It also generates the vertical clips for social. Annual billing is $24/mo; month-to-month is $35.

$49/mo
Transistor.fm
Professional · hosting

Your one upload generates the audio feed for every podcast app and auto-posts the full video to YouTube. Unlimited shows on one bill, so a second AES show later costs nothing extra.

$15/mo
Calendly
Standard · booking

Guests self-book a recording slot; it detects their timezone and emails them the Riverside link, the prep one-pager, and reminders automatically. One shared booking calendar avoids double-booking.

How an episode reaches the world

Guaranteed path (set up once, runs itself)

Audio: Transistor → one RSS feed → Apple Podcasts + Spotify + every podcast app, automatically on each publish.   Video: Transistor auto-posts the full episode to YouTube, the primary home for video discovery (about a third of US podcast viewing).

Bonus path (only if it's live on the account)

Native video on Apple Podcasts and Spotify ships through Transistor's HLS video distribution, which is still rolling out in 2026. Treat it as a bonus we switch on if available during setup, never something the launch depends on. YouTube is the guaranteed video home.

"Record once, clip many"

The full edited episode is the master. From it: the audio track fans out to every podcast app, the full video lands on YouTube, and Descript cuts 2–4 vertical clips per episode for YouTube Shorts, LinkedIn, and Instagram. Audio still reaches roughly twice the weekly US audience of video, so the show never waits on video to ship.

The one dollar that matters most

The microphone. If anything has to flex, keep the MV7+ and trade down elsewhere. We run it over USB-C in "Auto Level" mode with light denoise and reverb off; an XLR upgrade path exists later but is not needed now.

Guest uplift kit (optional, for marquee guests only)

Guests are the weakest link in remote A/V; a senior operator on a bad laptop webcam will be the most-watched face in the episode. For high-value guests, offer to ship a tiny uplift kit ahead of the record: a clip-on light and a USB mic or lav (about $25–60 total). Everyone else uses what they have; the platform protects the audio.

The Production WorkflowMechanisms in Motion

Record clean, edit by text, ship to spec

Quality is decided at record time, not in the edit. Get separate local tracks and matched settings on the way in, then Descript does the heavy lifting on the way out. Two rules prevent almost every disaster: confirm the real mic is selected, and never close the tab until uploads finish.

Session-day checklist

  • Both hosts join 10 min early in Chrome
  • In Riverside, select the MV7+ as the mic (not the laptop default)
  • Confirm "separate tracks / local recording" is ON
  • Headphones on for everyone (kills echo)
  • Both hosts run a QuickTime audio backup
  • Hit Record, say the slate ("Episode N, take 1"), pause 3 seconds silent for sync
  • Record the conversation
  • At the end, STAY on the page until every track shows 100% uploaded
If a guest's audio is rough

Ask them, live: move closer to the screen, turn off fans/AC, put on headphones. Do it in the warm-up minutes, not in the edit.

The Descript edit (4–6 hrs early, 2–3 later)

  1. Import the multitrack; confirm one track per speaker
  2. Apply Studio Sound per track at 60–80% (not 100%, it gets robotic)
  3. Edit by deleting text; then Remove Filler Words, keeping a few natural ones
  4. Run Automatic Multicam, then fix the obvious miscuts
  5. Add captions (auto-synced from the transcript)
  6. Generate 2–4 vertical clips; keep the best; hook in first 2 seconds
  7. Export video for YouTube and audio for Transistor

Export to spec: the same numbers every time

Loudness: −14 LUFS integrated
True peak: −1.0 dBTP (prevents distortion)
Video: 1080p, 30fps, H.264 MP4 → YouTube
Audio: stereo MP3/AAC, 48kHz → Transistor
Frame rate: 30fps locked across both kits + guests
Same audio bed in the video and the podcast file

Pre-publish QA gate (3 checks, every episode): (1) confirm the export reads −14 LUFS / −1.0 dBTP; (2) spot-check lip-sync at the start, middle, and final minute of the video; (3) confirm captions track the audio at the end of the file. Drift and loudness errors are invisible until a listener hits them on Spotify.

Scheduling & Guest OpsMechanisms in Motion

Guests self-book; the system preps them

One Calendly event and one prep doc run the entire guest journey. Carla sources and invites the guest; the guest books their own slot; Calendly handles timezones and sends everything they need. The tech check happens in the first five minutes of the recording.

The one Calendly event

  • Name: "Mechanisms in Motion: Recording"
  • Length: 75 min (60 of conversation + warm-up + slack)
  • Buffers: 15 min before and after
  • Location: the Riverside studio link, pasted as the meeting location so every booking carries it
  • Availability: a dedicated block, Tue/Thu mornings Pacific, capped at 1–2 recordings/day; no Saturdays
  • Intake questions: headline/title, LinkedIn or bio link, one Amazon-mechanism story they want to tell
  • Owner: one shared booking calendar; the co-host is a guest on the invite

The prep one-pager (one doc, reused)

  • The show premise and the "Day 1 Energy" tagline
  • The 5–6 question arc for their episode
  • Format: conversational, ~60 min, video on
  • The 4-line tech checklist: laptop + Chrome, headphones on, wired internet or sit near the router, close Slack/email/notifications
  • "We record locally, so a dropped connection won't ruin the audio. If you glitch, keep talking."
  • What to bring: one or two stories, a metric or two

The guest journey, end to end

CARLA INVITES
Warm outreach; sends the Calendly link.
GUEST BOOKS
Picks a slot; Calendly sends link + prep doc + reminders.
RECORD DAY
5-min device check, then record. Hosts run the session checklist.
AFTER
Edit, publish on its scheduled Tuesday, send the guest their clips.

Release cadence

Record the 8 plus one hosts-only evergreen, then publish weekly on a fixed day ("new episodes every Tuesday"). Launch gate: do not launch until 4 episodes are fully edited and QA-passed; editing, not recording, is the real constraint. Never publish below a one-episode buffer; if it hits zero, pause the streak on purpose rather than ship unedited. Use Transistor's scheduled-publish to queue the season, and start booking Season 2 in mid-October so the show never cliffs.
