# How to publish an episode (and the 30-second quality check)

**What "publish" means:** uploading the finished episode to **Transistor**, which then sends the audio to every podcast app and the video to YouTube. Before you publish, you run a quick quality check so nothing embarrassing goes out.

**Jargon, decoded:**
- **QA gate:** "QA" is quality assurance. A "gate" is just a checkpoint you pass before publishing. Three quick checks, every episode.
- **Scheduled-publish:** instead of publishing live, you can tell Transistor "go live next Tuesday at 8am" and it does it automatically. This lets you load all your episodes at once and walk away.
- **Directory submission:** a one-time step (Rich owns it) where the show is registered with Apple Podcasts and Spotify so it appears in their apps. After that, new episodes show up on their own.

## The 30-second QA check (before every publish)

1. **Loudness:** confirm the exported file reads about **−14 LUFS** and a **−1.0 dBTP** peak. Descript shows a loudness number on export; if you used the standard preset, it's already right. (What these mean: [export settings](export-settings.md).)
2. **Lip-sync:** play the video at the start, the middle, and the last minute. Do the mouths match the words? If they drift apart, don't publish; fix it (see [Descript edit](descript-edit.md)).
3. **Captions:** scroll to the end and confirm the subtitles still match what's being said.

If all three pass, you're clear to publish.

## Publishing in Transistor

1. In Transistor, create a new episode and upload the finished file.
2. Add the title, the show notes, and the cover.
3. Either **publish now**, or set a **publish date** (scheduled-publish) so it goes live on its Tuesday automatically.
4. Confirm the video auto-posted to YouTube (or upload it by hand if needed).
5. Send the guest their clips and a thank-you.

**Keep a buffer:** always stay at least one finished episode ahead. If you ever run out, pause for a week with a short note rather than publishing something half-done.
